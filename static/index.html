Discrete energy bins

One energy (keV) and one integrated photon fluence per bin. Optional header: energy_keV,weight. Weights must be finite, nonnegative, and not all zero. Up to 10,000 bins. This input is integrated bin fluence; convert spectral density to bin fluence before importing.

Energy weighting multiplies photon fluence by photon energy. Reported mean energies always use photon-fluence weighting. No detector response or tube spectrum is assumed.

TRANSPARENT BY DESIGN

The physics, with its boundaries.

Explicit assumptions and traceable scientific data are part of every result.

Narrow-beam attenuation

T(E) = exp[−Σᵢ (μ/ρ)ᵢ(E) · ρᵢ · tᵢ / cos θᵢ]

Each layer is a homogeneous mixture. Elemental mass attenuation coefficients are combined by mass fraction, then multiplied by the effective bulk density and ray path. Thickness is entered normal to the layer surface; angles are measured from the surface normal. Removed photons include absorption and scattering out of the primary beam.

Composition and density

Mass, mole, and volume inputs are normalized. Mole fractions refer to the entered chemical formula units. Volume fractions require constituent densities. Measured bulk density already includes porosity; solid density and ideal additive-volume density are reduced by the specified pore fraction. Ideal density is an estimate: real glass and ceramic mixtures may exhibit nonadditive volume.

Energy grid and attenuation lengths

Cross sections use the Elam data implemented in XrayDB, evaluated from 1 to 800 keV. The sweep adds samples on either side of reported absorption edges. The reference energy and imported spectrum energies are evaluated directly. HVL, TVL, and attenuation length describe path length in each homogeneous material at the reference energy. Target scaling changes all layer thicknesses proportionally.

Scope of the calculation

Independent-atom mixture coefficients omit chemical-state near-edge structure, coherent interfaces, and microstructure-dependent transport. Edge brackets locate tabulated steps; they do not predict XANES/EXAFS. Detector response is not included. This model does not simulate buildup, multiple scattering, secondary fluorescence transport, dose deposition, diffraction, interference, or X-ray reflectivity. It does not resolve chemical near-edge structure. Removal from the primary beam is not energy absorption. Layers in this model commute; their order does not change total transmission. Parallel physical interfaces share a common incidence angle; per-layer angles represent independently oriented segments.
