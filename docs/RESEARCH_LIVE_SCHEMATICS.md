## Guinier fit interval

**Data and attribution.** Credit Jill Trewhella, Patrice Vachette and Andreas Haahr Larsen, *Benchmarking predictive methods for small-angle X-ray scattering from atomic coordinates of proteins using maximum likelihood consensus data*, IUCrJ **11** (2024), 762–779, DOI 10.1107/S205225252400486X. [Paper, §2.2 and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1107/S205225252400486X) · [SASDUE4 deposition](https://www.sasbdb.org/data/SASDUE4/) · [Deposited DAT](https://www.sasbdb.org/media/intensities_files/SASDUE4.dat) · [Reuse terms](https://www.sasbdb.org/about/) · [Offline fixture](../tests/data/sasdue4.dat)

**Reproduce the fixed window.** From the fixture, skip blank and `#` lines, split on whitespace and join the first two columns with commas for equal weights or the first three for weighted fitting. Join all 223 data rows with newlines as `intensity_data`. Supply `qmin_inv_angstrom=0.0` and `qmax_inv_angstrom=0.083`. Use the DAT q values directly in Å⁻¹ (the website plot uses nm⁻¹). Both runs select 101 rows inclusively, from 0.0103382 to 0.08249249 Å⁻¹. On the q² plot those actual endpoints are 0.00010687837924 and 0.0068050109064001 Å⁻². Any handles marking the requested bounds on that axis belong at 0 and 0.006889 Å⁻². [Fixed-window regression, `test_sasdue4_fixed_window`](../tests/test_guinier.py) · [Returned arrays and bounds](../xray_workbench/guinier.py)

**Independent expectations.** With the selected rows set `x=q²`, `y=ln(I)` and solve the two-column system `[1, x] [b, m] = y` by SciPy least squares. For weighted fitting, multiply each design row and its target by `I/sigma` before solving. This represents independent first-order log errors. Recover Rg in Å as √(−3m) and I(0) in input intensity units as exp(b). The repository test checks both fits against constants from this independent regression. [Fixed-window regression and tolerances](../tests/test_guinier.py)

- **Equal weights.** Rg 14.444679810730591 Å, I(0) 1.0043843705307729, slope −69.549591611509314 Å², RMS log residual 0.008709117253566344, reduced χ² null. The fitted endpoints give qRg ≈ 0.149332 and 1.191578. [Fixed-window test](../tests/test_guinier.py)
- **With sigma.** Rg 14.499421700112727 Å, I(0) 1.0060907514343314, slope −70.077743212566602 Å², RMS log residual 0.008797655407313301, reduced log-space χ² 1.230366676552294 (99 degrees of freedom). The fitted endpoints give qRg ≈ 0.149898 and 1.196093. [Fixed-window test](../tests/test_guinier.py)
- **Probe row 51 of 101, counting from one.** The fixture supplies q = 0.02960806 Å⁻¹, I = 0.9460863 and σ = 0.002452136. Both plots use x = 0.0008766372169636 Å⁻². The observation is ln(I) = −0.05542148787547206. The independent weighted fit gives −0.05536047999880821 there, so the signed residual (observed minus fitted) is −0.00006100787666385443. Equal weights give a fitted value of −0.05659497325187279 and a positive residual of 0.001173485376400725. Use the same row index in the returned marker, line, residual and text readout. Keep the markers visible where the line overlaps them. [Fixture](../tests/data/sasdue4.dat) · [Fit and residual plot contract](../xray_workbench/guinier.py)

**Published comparison.** Table 2's lysozyme row gives AUTORG Rg = 14.54 ± 0.01 Å over qRg = 0.17–1.30 for the updated consensus. Its interval selection differs from the fixed-window regressions above. qRg is dimensionless. The 1.3 upper guide applies to compact globular particles. Interval choice also requires inspecting residual curvature and low-q effects such as aggregation. [Trewhella et al., §2.2 and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11364021/#sec2.2)

## Parallel-beam tomography view spacing

**Sampling convention.** R. A. Crowther, D. J. DeRosier and A. Klug, *The reconstruction of a three-dimensional structure from projections and its application to electron microscopy*, Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A **317** (1970), 319–340, DOI 10.1098/rspa.1970.0119, gives the Fourier sampling basis. M. Du et al., *Upscaling X-ray nanoimaging to macroscopic specimens*, J. Appl. Cryst. **54** (2021), 386–401, DOI 10.1107/S1600576721000194, §3.2, Eqs. (6)–(7), specifies 2D/d samples across a support of diameter D for full spatial period d, then π/2 times that many angular views. For ideal parallel beams and a centered circular support, round up once to N = ceil(πD/d). The object-space pitch p separately requires d ≥ 2p. [Crowther et al.](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1970.0119) · [Du et al., Fig. 5 and Eqs. (6)–(7)](https://doi.org/10.1107/S1600576721000194) · [Sampling implementation](../xray_workbench/tomo_sampling.py)

**Independent example.** J. K. Rogalinski et al., *Time-resolved 3D imaging opportunities with XMPI at ForMAX*, J. Synchrotron Rad. **33** (2026), 417–428, DOI 10.1107/S1600577525011038. Section 2.2.2 reports a 20 µm Photron sensor pitch with a 5× objective, giving 4 µm effective pitch. Section 2.3, Eq. (1), estimates roughly 1,600 conventional projections across 1,024 pixels. Their XMPI instrument acquires three simultaneous projections. The test constructs a 4,096 µm support from 1,024 × 4 µm and an 8 µm target period from 2 × 4 µm. The paper links a Zenodo deposit under Data availability. The benchmark reuses only the published numerical example and requires no projection files. Archive reuse terms were not established here. [Rogalinski et al., §§2.2.2–2.3 and Data availability](https://journals.iucr.org/s/issues/2026/02/00/gy5083/index.html) · [`test_formax_crowther_example`](../tests/test_tomo_sampling.py)

- **Angular shortfall.** Supply `{"diameter_um":4096,"resolution_um":8,"pixel_pitch_um":4,"planned_views":1600}`. The unrounded requirement is 512π ≈ 1608.495438637974. Expect minimum 1609, minimum-count step 180/1609 ≈ 0.11187072715972654°, planned step 0.1125°, shortfall 9, angular minimum unmet. The pixel floor is 8 µm and supports the target. [ForMAX test](../tests/test_tomo_sampling.py)
- **Angular sufficiency with coarse pitch.** Change just `planned_views` to 1609 and `pixel_pitch_um` to 5. Both schedules now have 1609 views and shortfall 0. The 10 µm pixel floor exceeds the requested 8 µm period. Show the two outcomes independently. Omitting or blanking the optional plan removes its comparison fields and leaves the minimum schedule available. [Coarse-pitch and blank-plan tests](../tests/test_tomo_sampling.py)
- **Display geometry.** Use the returned count and step for the selected schedule. With zero-based index k, the angle is k × step for 0 ≤ k < N on [0°, 180°). In the 1600-view plan, k = 0, 1, 800, 1599 gives 0°, 0.1125°, 90°, 179.8875°. In the minimum schedule, k = 1608 gives 180° − 180°/1609 ≈ 179.88812927284027°. Label any subset of drawn rays as illustrative and keep the selected angle exact. Circle size, arrow length and screen coordinates are drawing choices. The response supplies sampling scalars and an empty `plots` list. Projection intensities, a sinogram, reconstructed anatomy and achieved resolution would require additional evidence. [Sampling response](../xray_workbench/tomo_sampling.py) · [ForMAX test](../tests/test_tomo_sampling.py)

## Proposed browser acceptance checks

1. **Keyboard interval and probe.** With Tab and Shift+Tab reach both named bounds, Run, and the sample probe in a predictable order. Enter the SASDUE4 bounds by keyboard, run the weighted fit and move to row 51. Compare the textual q, measured value, fit and signed residual to the probe above and the same index in exported JSON. Arrow keys should move one sample, Home and End to the first and last samples. Probing should preserve the calculated fit. Request an interval with only two samples, check for the three-row error and cleared stale readouts/exports, then restore the valid interval and verify recovery. [Guinier tests](../tests/test_guinier.py) · [Existing marker and export audit](../tools/ui_audit.py)
2. **Keyboard view spacing.** From the 1600-view case, step the probe from k = 0 to 1, then to the final view with End. Compare the angle and view number to the half-open schedule above. Switch to the minimum schedule and verify its final angle and count. Enter the coarse-pitch case, then clear planned views. Check the textual angular and pixel statuses, removal of the planned overlay, visible focus and continued access to the minimum schedule. Trigger the scientific JSON export by keyboard and compare its scalars to the readout. The existing sampling tool offers JSON only. [Sampling tests](../tests/test_tomo_sampling.py) · [Export audit](../tools/ui_audit.py)
3. **Narrow screens, both themes.** Repeat with populated results at 375 × 812 and 320 × 812 CSS pixels. Check page width against viewport width and inspect the local bounds of labels, legend, readouts, errors and focus rings for clipping. Keep units and both interval endpoints readable, targets at least 24 × 24 CSS pixels, and text at least 12 px. Distinguish observed points, fitted line, selected ray and warning states with shapes or text as well as colour. Every hover or drag readout should have a keyboard-accessible text equivalent. Resize with a probe selected and confirm its sample or view index and numerical value persist. [Workspace audit viewports](../tools/workspace_ui_audit.py) · [Repository interface criteria](../AGENTS.md#interface-standards)
